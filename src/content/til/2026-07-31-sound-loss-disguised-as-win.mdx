A "loss disguised as a win" (LDW) is when a slot machine pays back less than was wagered — bet $10, win back $1 — but still plays the same celebratory winning sound. Brain scans of gambling addicts show the same reward regions light up for an LDW as for an actual win, because the sound arrives before the rational brain has time to do the subtraction.

A University of Waterloo study found that with the sound turned off, players correctly categorized these outcomes as losses. Turn the audio back on, and the same players slide right back into treating them as wins. The sound isn't decoration on top of the outcome — it's what determines how the outcome gets perceived in the first place.

This connects to Skinner's variable-reward pigeons: unpredictable payouts drive compulsive checking behavior, and sound is the delivery mechanism that makes each spin (or notification) feel like it might be the rewarding one, keeping people in the loop longer than the actual math would justify.
